Privacy

samfran · samfran.rudnam.com · updated November 16, 2026

samfran is a private app for two people: the restaurants they go to and one person's work shifts. Only people the owner invites can use it. Nothing in it is sold, shared with advertisers, or used to train anything.

What samfran keeps

  • Your email address and, if you use one, a hash of your password. If you sign in with Google, samfran receives your email address and Google's ID for your account.
  • What the two of you add: restaurants, visits, ratings and notes, and shifts (a code per day, such as 13F).
  • If you connect Google Calendar, a token that lets samfran update its own calendar in your account, stored encrypted.

Google Calendar

Connecting Google Calendar asks only for the permission to make secondary calendars and manage the events on them (calendar.app.created). samfran uses it to create one calendar of the shifts in your account and keep its events up to date. It can't see or change your other calendars or events. You can disconnect in Shifts › Settings, or remove samfran's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. samfran's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Photos of shift sheets

A photo of a shift sheet is read to find one person's row and is never stored. When Claude reads it through samfran's connector, that happens in your Claude account, under Anthropic's terms.

Where it's kept, and deleting it

samfran runs on Amazon Web Services in Sydney (ap-southeast-2), with nightly backups kept for 30 days. Deleting your account deletes your data; what you share with the other person stays theirs until they delete theirs.
